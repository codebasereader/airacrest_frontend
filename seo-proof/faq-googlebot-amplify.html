Dehydrated Banana Powder

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about quality, packaging, and export details for dehydrated banana powder.

They are two different products from the same fruit, and choosing between them is the first specification decision. Green banana powder is milled from unripe fruit harvested before starch converts to sugar: pale, neutral tasting, high in resistant starch, and used where fibre, gluten free structure, or slow release carbohydrate is the goal. Ripe banana powder carries the golden colour, aroma, and natural sweetness of the ripe fruit, and is used where banana flavour is the point: beverages, dairy, bakery, cereals. We supply both as separate grades with separate specifications, and we will quote the correct one once you tell us the application.

Resistant starch in green banana flour typically falls between 40 and 60 percent on a dry basis, but the honest answer is that this figure moves with harvest maturity and drying temperature. We do not publish a single headline number, because a number without a method behind it is meaningless. We agree a target range with you before production, specify the analytical method on the purchase contract, and report the actual result on the certificate of analysis for every lot. If your specification calls for a guaranteed minimum, tell us at enquiry stage so harvest timing can be planned for it.

This is where most low cost banana flour fails. Resistant starch is destroyed by heat: drum drying, extrusion, or aggressive hot air drying gelatinises the starch granule and the resistant starch fraction collapses, often to single digits, while the label still says green banana flour. Our green grade is dried below 60 degrees Celsius and the outcome is verified by testing rather than by trusting a process description. If a competing quotation is materially cheaper, ask that supplier for a resistant starch result on the delivered lot. The gap usually explains itself.

Yes, and this matters enough that we raise it before you ask. Ripe banana pulp is high in sugar and extremely sticky, so spray drying almost always requires a carrier, most commonly maltodextrin, making up a meaningful share of the finished powder. A supplier claiming pure spray dried banana powder with no carrier is either selling something else or not telling you the whole story. We declare the carrier type and inclusion level on every specification and certificate of analysis. The green flour, by contrast, is milled whole fruit and carries no additives. If you need a carrier free ripe product for a clean label programme, freeze dried is the honest route and we will quote it accordingly.

Spray dried is the commercial workhorse: good flavour retention, fine free flowing particle, and the most competitive cost per kilogram. Drum dried gives a slightly cooked, caramel leaning profile that some bakery buyers actually prefer, at lower cost again. Freeze dried retains the brightest fresh fruit character and colour but sits at a substantially higher price and suits premium retail or infant applications. There is no universally correct answer. Tell us the application and target landed cost and we will recommend honestly, including telling you when the cheaper process will serve you perfectly well.

Principally Cavendish and Nendran from South India, where year round availability keeps supply stable rather than seasonal. Cavendish gives the familiar profile most Western formulations expect and a paler, more neutral green flour. Nendran carries a firmer starch profile favoured in traditional flour applications. Regional varieties including Robusta and Poovan offer distinctive aroma profiles for specialty products. If you are matching an existing reference sample, send it to us and we will work backwards to the variety and process that reproduces it rather than quoting a standard grade and hoping it lands close.

For the green flour: moisture below 10 percent, particle size 80 to 100 mesh, colour off white to pale cream, neutral aroma, with ash, protein, and total dietary fibre reported per lot. For the ripe powder: moisture below 5 percent, 60 to 80 mesh, colour light cream to golden, characteristic sweet aroma, with total sugars, carrier content, and bulk density reported per lot. Bulk density matters more than buyers expect, because it drives both container fill and dry blending behaviour. Every consignment ships with a certificate of analysis against the agreed parameters, and we will build the specification sheet jointly with your technical team.

Cut banana browns quickly through enzymatic oxidation, and how a processor handles that window determines what you receive. Some use a sulphite dip, which controls colour effectively but introduces sulphur dioxide that must be declared and that many clean label buyers refuse. Others blanch, which controls colour but applies heat, and on the green grade that costs resistant starch. In the ripe grade, grey tones point to browning during holding and dark brown to excessive drying heat. We can supply either route, we will tell you plainly which compromise you are buying, and sulphur dioxide status is declared on every certificate of analysis.

The green grade goes to gluten free bakery, prebiotic and digestive health supplements, sports nutrition, infant and weaning foods, pet nutrition, and pasta manufacturers seeking a wheat replacement, with the resistant starch positioning commanding a clear premium in the United States. The ripe grade goes to smoothie and beverage premixes, flavoured dairy, bakery and confectionery, breakfast cereals and bars, and infant cereals. We quote the two grades and the two market types differently because the specification, documentation, and packaging expectations genuinely differ.

Twelve to eighteen months from production in unopened original packaging, stored below 25 degrees Celsius away from light and humidity. Both grades are hygroscopic; the ripe powder markedly more so because of its sugar content, and it will cake if it takes on moisture. For humid destinations we strongly recommend an aluminium foil laminate liner with a good moisture barrier rather than plain polyethylene, and we will specify it on the packing list. Once opened, decant into sealed containers and keep away from strongly aromatic stock.

Standard bulk packing is 25 kilogram multiwall paper bags with a food grade liner, foil laminate for the ripe grade, palletised and shrink wrapped. Smaller programmes are served with 10 or 20 kilogram foil laminate bags, and buyer branded retail packs are available from 100 grams upward. The ripe powder has a comparatively low bulk density, so container fill is limited by volume rather than weight and packaging format directly affects freight cost per kilogram. We will show you that calculation when we quote.

Both grades are generally classified under HS heading 1106.30, covering flour, meal, and powder of the products of Chapter 8, with Indian ITC HS line 11063090 commonly used. Classification can vary at destination, particularly where the product is presented as a supplement ingredient or where a spray dried powder carries significant carrier content. Final determination rests with your customs authority. We state the code on all shipping documents and will support your broker with product and process detail if a ruling is required.
